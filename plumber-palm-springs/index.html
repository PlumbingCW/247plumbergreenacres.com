From dairy farm to about 800 homes by August 1958

Palm Springs was chartered as a village on May 31, 1957, on land with dairy farm roots. By August 1958 about 800 homes had been built there. That is a lot of houses in a short stretch, so a large share of the village was plumbed in one burst with whatever was standard at the time.

Those houses are now well past the age when original lines can be trusted. Homes of the late 1950s were commonly built on concrete slabs, with supply and drain pipes under the floor where nobody sees them. A warm spot on the tile, the sound of water with every fixture off, or a meter that keeps turning are the usual signs, and slab leak repair starts with leak detection to pinpoint the break.

Drain lines from that era also tend to be near the end of their service life, and mature trees in older yards put roots right beside them. If a main line backs up more than once, a sewer inspection with a camera is a better next step than another round of snaking.

Fix the old line or replace it?

Nobody wants a repipe they do not need, and nobody wants to pay for the same repair three times. This is how we weigh it in houses of this age.

A spot repair makes sense when

  • One leak, one section, and the rest of the pipe tests sound.
  • The failed piece is easy to reach in an attic or wall cavity.
  • The pipe material is still serviceable and not flaking inside.
  • You plan to sell or remodel soon and want to limit the scope.

Replacing the line makes sense when

  • A second or third leak shows up within a short period.
  • The pipe is rusted or scaled through along its run.
  • Pressure is poor at several fixtures, not just one.
  • Repair access would mean opening the slab more than once.

A whole-house job is a repiping project, and we explain what the scope would be before you decide.

What we are called out for in 1950s Palm Springs houses

The problems cluster, because the houses were built in clusters.

Water heaters at the end of the road

A tank that has run for a decade or more often leaks at the base or trips the breaker. When the tank itself has failed, water heater replacement is the repair.

Kitchen drains and disposals

Older kitchens often have undersized drain runs that grease clings to. A garbage disposal repair visit frequently turns up a partly closed drain behind it.

Toilets that run or rock

Worn flappers, cracked fill valves and loose flange bolts waste water and loosen the seal. Toilet repair is usually a short visit.

Wet yards and soft spots

A patch of lawn that stays soggy in dry weather can point to a failed line between the meter and the house. That is a job for water service line repair.

Before we arrive in Palm Springs

Most houses here are single-story and slab-built, so water spreads across the floor quickly. These four steps limit the damage.

Close the main shutoff

Look near the meter at the front of the property or where the line enters the house. Turn it clockwise until it stops. If you cannot find it, say so on the call.

Cut power to wet rooms

Switch off breakers only if you can reach the panel without standing in water. Keep cords and appliances away from the wet floor.

Lift what the water can reach

Move rugs, boxes and furniture off the slab. Towels at door thresholds stop water from running into the next room.
