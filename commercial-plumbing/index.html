After-hours commercial plumbing emergency

A flooded kitchen at night or a leaking ceiling over a stockroom cannot wait for Monday. Our phone line is answered around the clock, and we offer same-day appointments when available. Before anyone arrives, close the building's main water valve if you can reach it, stop using drains, and move stock and electronics away from water.

Shut off power to wet areas only if you can do so safely and the panel is dry. Call us and describe what is happening, including whether it is a leak, a backup or a loss of water.

Problems we see in commercial buildings

Commercial plumbing handles more fixtures and heavier use than a house, so failures show up differently.

Overflowing floor drains

A floor drain that rises when sinks run points to a blocked branch or main. It is common in kitchens and restrooms with heavy daily use.

Grease buildup

Restaurant kitchens send fats down the line, and grease hardens on the pipe wall. Slow sinks and odor are early signs.

Leaks over ceilings and stock

Water stains on a ceiling tile or a wet wall often trace to a supply line or a drain above. Finding the source early limits damage.

Restrooms out of service

Constant flushing, wipes and stuck fixtures take restrooms down, and a closed restroom can force a closed business.

Grease and hydro jetting

Cable machines open a hole through grease, but they leave a coating behind, and the line narrows again. Hydro jetting uses high-pressure water to strip that coating and return the pipe to its full bore.

For a restaurant, a jetting pass on a regular schedule can be the difference between a clear line and a repeat emergency. We recommend based on what the first clearing brings up.

Backflow testing, grease traps and storm drains

These three are common requests, and each is covered here as a service rather than as a separate page.

Backflow testing

Backflow assemblies protect the public water supply from contamination. Many are tested on a regular schedule, and callers often mention a notice from the utility or a locked valve for a missed test. We can test assemblies, so call with the device location and any notice you received.

Grease trap cleaning

Restaurants and kitchens use traps to catch fats before they reach the sewer. A full trap smells, backs up and passes grease downstream. We can pump and clean a trap, and we check the lines on either side.

Storm drain cleaning

Parking-lot and yard drains collect leaves, sand and trash, and a blocked one floods a lot during Florida downpours. Callers have asked about stuck grates and debris as well. We clear the drain and the line behind it.

Working with property managers and tenants

Rental properties and multi-unit buildings add people to the call. A tenant may report a leak, a manager approves the work, and an owner may handle payment. We can speak with whoever has authority, and we explain what we find in plain terms so the decision can be made quickly.

If you manage several units, tell us which one is affected and whether the problem may be shared with neighbors. Shared lines mean a backup may have started elsewhere.

Have this ready when you call

A few details save time on the phone and help us bring the right equipment.

  • Street address and the unit or suite number.
  • The type of building, such as a restaurant, office or plaza unit.
  • What is wrong: leak, backup, no water or no hot water.
  • Whether you have shut off the main valve.
  • Where the cleanout, meter or shutoff is, if you know.
  • Who has authority to approve work, and how to reach them.
