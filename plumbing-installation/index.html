What we install

Installation calls come from every part of a home. Some people have bought a new sink or tub and need it hooked up. Others are adding a bathroom, moving a kitchen wall, or running water to a washer and dryer that never had connections.

Whatever the project, call us with a short description. It helps to know what you have, what you want, and whether walls or floors are already open.

  • Kitchen sinks, bar sinks and utility sinks.
  • Tubs, showers and shower valves.
  • Washer boxes and laundry supply and drain lines.
  • Shutoff stops and main shutoff valves.
  • Water pressure regulators and outdoor hose bibs.
  • Rough-in for new bathrooms and remodels.

Remodel rough-in, step by step in the walls

Rough-in is the part you never see once the drywall goes up. Supply lines get their stub-outs, drains get sloped to the main, and vents go through the roof or tie into existing ones. We set stub heights to your fixture specs so a tub spout, a vanity or a wall-hung toilet lands where the design says it should.

The key is planning. Choose fixtures before the rough-in, because stub locations differ from one model to another. If you change your mind afterward, it can mean reopening the wall.

Adding a bathroom or laundry line

A new bathroom or laundry needs a drain with enough slope and a vent, plus hot and cold supply. Slab-on-grade homes may need floor cutting to reach the drain, so we check the layout first and tell you what to expect.

Valves, regulators and outdoor spigots

A good shutoff valve is the cheapest insurance you can install. Every fixture should have its own stop, and the house should have a main valve that turns freely. If either one is stuck, replace it before something fails.

A pressure regulator sits at the main entry and keeps house pressure steady. Weak, noisy or fluctuating pressure can point to a worn regulator, and a plumber can test it with a gauge on a hose bib. Outdoor spigots should have a shutoff, be accessible, and be tied into a good supply line, not a corroded stub.

Permits, licensing and the building department

In Greenacres, permit applications are accepted only electronically, work must be done by a licensed contractor or an exempt owner-builder, and inspections must be scheduled by 3:00 pm. Ask the City of Greenacres building department about your project.

Before you hire anyone

Palm Beach County gives homeowners a few checks.

  • Confirm the Certificate of Competency in the county licensed contractor search at pbc.gov/pzb.
  • Verify state licensing at myfloridalicense.com.
  • Ask for current proof of insurance.
  • A Business Tax Receipt is not a license.
  • Be cautious about large upfront payments or oral-only deals.

Using an unlicensed contractor can bring code enforcement fines to the property owner.

New work and the code

New fixtures, lines and rough-in work follow the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which Florida builds on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), which rests on the IPC. Inspectors look at that new work, so the details need to be right the first time.

Ask the City of Greenacres building department which permits apply to your project. Inspections there are scheduled by 3:00 pm.
