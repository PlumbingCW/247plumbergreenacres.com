No hot water, or water pooling around the tank?

A dead water heater always seems to quit at the worst moment, and a leaking one can ruin a garage floor before you notice. Our line is answered around the clock, so call us any hour. While you wait, close the cold water valve on the pipe above the tank and switch the heater off (the breaker for an electric or hybrid unit, the gas control knob for a gas unit).

Keep people and pets away from standing water near a plugged-in appliance, and stop running hot taps. If you ever smell gas, skip the knob entirely: leave the house, then call the gas utility or 911 from outside.

What callers describe, and what it usually points to

Most repair calls start with a plain description rather than a part name. These are the patterns we hear most often, with the usual suspect behind each.

Lukewarm or no hot water

On an electric tank this is often a failed lower element or a tripped high-limit switch. On a gas tank it points to a pilot that went out or a dirty burner.

Water around the base

A wet drain pan can mean a loose fitting or a weeping relief valve. A steady drip from the tank body itself usually means the steel shell has rusted through.

Popping or rumbling

Sediment settles on the tank floor, traps steam bubbles and makes the tank knock as it heats. A flush often quiets it, but a heavy crust can shorten the tank's life.

Breaker trips again

A breaker that trips right after you reset it often points to a shorted element or damaged wiring. Do not keep resetting it, since repeated tripping can be a sign of a real fault.

A leak from a fitting is a repair. A leak from the tank body is the end of that tank, and we will say so plainly rather than sell you parts for a lost cause.

Gas water heater: pilot, ignition and venting

A gas unit that will not stay lit usually has a worn thermocouple, a dirty pilot assembly or a failing gas control valve. People often tell us the pilot went out, or that pressing the ignition button does nothing. Relighting is simple in theory, but a pilot that keeps dying is a symptom, and the cause matters.

We also check the flue and draft on every gas repair, because a blocked or backdrafting vent sends combustion gases into the house. If you smell gas at any point, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911. Do not flip switches or hunt for the leak yourself.

Safety first on any gas call

We will not relight or adjust a gas appliance when you have reported a gas odor until the gas utility has cleared the house. That order protects you, and it is not negotiable.

Electric and hybrid heaters: elements, thermostats and breakers

A standard electric tank has two heating elements and two thermostats stacked behind small access panels. The upper element carries the first heat, and the lower one does most of the work afterward. When one fails, you typically get short, lukewarm showers instead of a total outage, which is why many homes live with the problem for weeks.

Hybrid units add a heat pump on top, which makes the diagnosis different. They carry error codes, a condensate drain that can clog and an electric backup mode. Some callers ask whether we work on hybrids at all, and the answer is yes, with a diagnosis first and a clear price before any parts go in.

  • Thermostat set too low or reset trip button popped.
  • Element tests open or shorted to the tank.
  • Wiring discolored at the terminals.
  • Condensate drain on a hybrid blocked with slime.
  • Breaker undersized or weakened by repeated tripping.

The anode rod is the cheap part that protects the expensive part

Inside every steel tank hangs a sacrificial anode rod that corrodes in place of the tank wall. Once it is eaten away, the tank itself starts to rust. Swapping the rod early is a modest job, and one callers ask about directly, while waiting until the tank leaks means the rod no longer matters.

Ask us to inspect the rod during a flush if your tank is getting older, since a rod that is mostly bare steel has done its job.

What insurance may and may not cover

A sudden failure may be treated differently from slow, long-term leakage, and policies differ. Check your policy, call your insurer and take photos of the damage before anything is moved.

Tank maintenance that cuts down on repeat calls

Most homeowners can check the temperature setting, look at the drain pan and test the relief valve lever without tools. A yearly flush clears sediment, which also helps electric elements last longer. If your tank is on a Lake Worth Road side street or out toward Jog Road, the age of the home does not change the routine.

Palm Beach County Water Utilities uses chloramine as its disinfectant and switches to free chlorine briefly twice a year. If the water suddenly smells different after one of those switches, that is a utility event, so ask PBCWUD before assuming your heater is at fault. When a unit is simply past saving, our installation page covers the planned route, and the tankless page covers the other option.

Repairs, relief valves and the code

Water heater work is covered by the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which Florida builds on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), built on the IPC. The temperature and pressure relief valve is a safety part, so we treat a dripping one as a repair to take seriously and not a nuisance.

A like-for-like swap in Greenacres has its own permit forms, covered on the water heater replacement page. A simple part repair is a different matter, so ask the building department if you are unsure.
