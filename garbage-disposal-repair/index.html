Safety first, every time

Never put your hand into a disposal, even when it is switched off. Flip the wall switch off and unplug the unit or turn off its breaker before you touch anything, and use tongs or pliers to pull out objects you can see.

Kitchen sink backed up or disposal leaking?

A disposal that is leaking under the sink or sending water up the other side of a double basin needs attention quickly, and our 24/7 line is open for it. Stop running the dishwasher and the faucet, because both drain through the same trap. Put a bowl or bucket under the leak, pull the plug from the outlet under the sink, and keep the area dry.

When you are ready, call us and say where the water is showing up. A leak from the top flange, the side near the dishwasher hose, or the bottom of the housing point to different fixes.

Symptoms and what they usually mean

Callers tend to describe the same few problems with their disposal.

Humming but not spinning

The motor has power but the flywheel is stuck. A wedged bone, fruit pit or utensil is the usual reason.

Completely silent

No hum and no spin often means a tripped overload. Check the red reset button on the bottom and the outlet or breaker.

Leaking underneath

Water at the top is the sink flange seal. Water at the side is the dishwasher hose or drain elbow. Water from the bottom or the body means the housing has failed.

Slow to drain or standing water

Food buildup in the trap or the pipe beyond the disposal. The disposal may be fine and the line is the problem.

Bad smell

Food residue clinging to the grinding chamber and splash guard. A rubber baffle that has gone slimy often holds the odor.

Metal grinding or rattling

A spoon or small object is inside, or the grinding parts have worn out. Do not run it until it is checked.

Clearing a jam the safe way

Power off

Switch off the wall switch and unplug the unit, or flip the breaker. This is not optional.

Use the hex-key slot

Most disposals have a socket at the center of the bottom. Insert the small hex wrench and work it back and forth until the flywheel turns freely.

Remove the item

Look inside with a flashlight and pull out any object with tongs, never fingers.

Press reset

Wait a few minutes, press the red button on the bottom, restore power and run cold water. If it hums or trips again, stop and call.

Disposals are not meant to last forever, and when the housing leaks, replacement is almost always the answer.

Putting in a new disposal

A new unit means removing the old one from the mounting ring, cleaning the sink opening, fitting a new flange with plumber's putty, and locking the disposal onto the ring. We reconnect the drain elbow to the trap and the dishwasher hose, and if the dishwasher is hooked up, we knock out the plug inside the inlet so it can drain.

A few things affect the job: whether there is an outlet and switch under or beside the sink, the horsepower and size you choose, and whether the trap arm lines up with the new outlet height. A unit with a cord and plug is simpler than one that has to be hardwired. If there has never been a disposal under the sink, say so when you call, since wiring and venting matter.

What should and should not go down

Fine in small amounts

  • Soft scraps and small bits of cooked food, with cold water running.
  • Citrus peel in small pieces for a fresh smell.
  • A few ice cubes now and then to knock off residue.

Keep out of the disposal

  • Grease, fat and oil, which harden further down the line.
  • Coffee grounds, eggshells, pasta and rice, which pack into sludge.
  • Potato peels, celery and other stringy or starchy fibers.
  • Bones, fruit pits and anything not food.

Most kitchen line clogs we see start with grease and starch settling in the trap arm and beyond. If the water stands in both basins even after the disposal runs, our drain cleaning work clears the line behind it.

What insurance may and may not cover

Policies usually treat sudden water damage differently from slow leaks, and many do not cover the disposal as an appliance. If water reached cabinets or flooring, photograph it and call your insurer to ask.

Disposals, wiring and plumbing rules

The drain connections on a disposal follow the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which Florida builds on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), built on the IPC. The electrical side is covered by a separate code, so treat the two jobs separately.

Replacing a cord-type unit is routine. If new wiring or a changed drain is involved, ask the City of Greenacres building department about permits.
