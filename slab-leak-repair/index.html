Signs the leak is under your floor

Many Florida homes are built on a concrete slab with pipes running in or under it. A leak there is out of sight, but it leaves evidence.

Hot spot on the floor

A patch of tile or wood that feels warm underfoot often means a hot water line is leaking beneath it.

Sound of running water

You hear a hiss or trickle with every faucet and appliance off. Put your ear near the floor in a quiet room.

Rising water bill

Usage climbs and you cannot explain it. The meter turns with everything shut off.

Damp or lifting flooring

Carpet that feels wet, tile grout that darkens, wood that cups, or baseboards that swell along one wall.

Low hot water pressure

A hot line leaking under the slab can reduce pressure at the water heater side while cold flow stays normal.

Cracks or wet spot outside

Wet soil along the foundation, or a puddle against the house where nothing explains it.

Confirming a slab leak before anything is cut

Callers often say a bill went up or a part of the floor feels warm, and they want to know if it is under the slab. We confirm that first. A pressure test checks whether the lines hold, and closing the house valve at the meter tells us whether the loss is inside or outside.

From there we pinpoint with listening and thermal methods. The goal is a spot repair or a reroute that opens as little floor as possible. A guess that opens the wrong tile costs you twice. For the broader search process see leak detection.

Your repair options under a slab

There are three common answers, and which one fits depends on what we find.

OptionWhat it involvesBest fit
Spot repair.Break the concrete at the pinpointed spot, cut out the failed section, replace it, close the floor.One leak on a line that is otherwise sound.
Reroute.Abandon the leaking line under the slab and run a new one through walls or the attic.A line with more than one failure or one that is hard to reach.
Repipe.Replace the affected lines or the whole house supply, see repipingWidespread pitting or repeated leaks on the same system.

A reroute avoids reopening the same floor later. A spot repair is quicker and costs less now. We show you the leak and the pipe condition so you can pick.

What a concrete break-in actually involves

Protect and mark

We clear the area, cover nearby floors, and mark the cut line from the pinpoint.

Cut and remove

We saw-cut the flooring and slab, remove the concrete in pieces, and expose the pipe. Dust control and a wet-vac help contain the mess.

Repair or replace the pipe

The failed section is cut out and a new piece is joined in. We pressure test before anything is closed.

Patch the slab

The opening is refilled with concrete. Flooring replacement is a separate job and not part of this visit unless we agree on it.

What insurance may and may not cover

Policies handle slab leaks in very different ways. Some cover the damage the water causes but not the pipe itself. Some cover the access cost, such as breaking and patching the slab, and some exclude it. Check your policy, call your insurer, and document the wet floors and meter readings.

Slab repairs and the code

Rerouting or replacing lines after a slab leak falls under the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which Florida builds on the International Plumbing Code (IPC). The IPC governs the new pipe, its supports and its connections.

Breaking a slab and rerouting piping is not a casual job. Ask the City of Greenacres building department about permits, and confirm your contractor holds a license before signing.
