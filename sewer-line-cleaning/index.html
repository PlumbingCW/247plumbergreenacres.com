The main line is backed up

Water coming up in the tub when the washer drains is a main line problem, and it will not fix itself. Our 24/7 line is answered around the clock, so call whenever it starts. Until we arrive, stop using every drain, because water you send down has nowhere to go but back into the house.

If sewage is already on the floor, keep children and pets away and stay off wet electrical cords. Close the main water valve if the washer or a toilet tank is still refilling. Take photos of the damage for your insurer before you clean up.

How to tell it is the main line and not one sink

A single clogged fixture is a branch problem. A main line problem shows up in several places at once, and the pattern tells us where the blockage sits.

Several fixtures slow together

A shower that drains slowly while the toilet bowl also rises points to the shared line. One sink on its own does not.

Gurgling floor drain or toilet

Air pushed back through a floor drain or bubbling in a toilet after the washer runs means flow is blocked downstream.

Lowest drain backs up first

Sewage tends to surface at the lowest opening, such as a shower or tub on the ground level. That drain shows the blockage is beyond the branch lines.

Sewer odor with slow flow

A foul smell in a bathroom that comes and goes with slow drains can mean the line is partly blocked, not only that a trap has dried out.

Where the cleanout is and why it matters

A cleanout is a capped pipe, usually white or black plastic about four inches wide, that gives a machine direct access to the main line. Many homes have one in the yard near the house wall or close to the property line, and some have one beside the foundation or in the garage.

Do not open a cleanout with sewage backed up behind it. The cap may release a surge of wastewater. Leave it capped and tell us where it sits. If you cannot find one, we work from a toilet or other access point instead.

Cable machine or hydro jetting

What happens on a main line visit

Locate access

We find the cleanout, or pull a toilet if there is none, so the machine can enter the main line.

Run the cable

A powered cable with a cutting head feeds toward the blockage. We note the distance where it stops, which hints at the cause.

Clear and flush

Once the clog breaks, we run the line until water moves freely, then test the fixtures one at a time.

Explain what we found

We tell you what came out, how far it was, and whether a camera is worth doing next.

When the roots come back

If a line clears and clogs again within months, roots are a common reason. Cutting them back restores flow, but the opening that let them in stays open, and roots regrow toward the moisture. Some lines need cleaning on a schedule, and some are better fixed once.

A sewer inspection with a camera shows where the roots enter and how many joints are affected. If one joint is the problem, our sewer line repair page explains the lining and spot repair options that close it.

What you can do and what needs a machine

Safe to try yourself

  • Stop all water use right away.
  • Close the main water valve if a tank is refilling.
  • Move rugs and valuables off the floor.
  • Photograph standing water for your insurer.

Leave it to us

  • Opening a cleanout with sewage behind it.
  • Running chemical drain products in a full line.
  • Feeding rented cable into a main line.
  • Pressure-washing a pipe you cannot see.

What insurance may and may not cover

Policies treat sewer backup differently. Some include it only with an endorsement, and gradual causes such as roots may be excluded. Check with your insurer and keep photos of the damage.

For your real number, call us and say which drains are slow.

A cleaning is not a rebuild

Cleaning a main line does not change the pipe, so the code mostly matters if repairs follow. Those repairs fall under the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which Florida builds on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), which is based on the IPC.

If the cleaning shows a defect, you will hear about the repair options and any permit step before we go further.
