A hidden leak is urgent when the meter will not stop

Hidden leaks do their damage quietly, which is why a call about one often starts with a bill or a meter. If water is actively pooling or the meter is spinning fast with every fixture off, shut the main valve, and call our 24/7 line. Our line is answered around the clock, and we book same-day appointments when available.

Callers describe it in a few ways. The water bill has crept up for months. The utility sent a leak alert. A toilet keeps running on its own. The yard has a soggy patch in dry weather. All of them point to the same first question: is the water leaking before the house, inside it, or after a fixture?

Test your own meter in ten minutes

Turn everything off

Close all faucets, stop the washer and dishwasher, and make sure no one flushes. Check that the ice maker is not mid-cycle.

Read the meter

Open the meter box and look at the dial or the low-flow indicator, the small triangle or wheel that spins at the slightest flow. Note the numbers.

Wait and watch

Leave everything off for ten to fifteen minutes. If the indicator still turns or the numbers advance, water is escaping somewhere.

Shut the house valve and look again

Close the main valve at the house. If the meter stops, the leak is inside the house or on the house side. If it keeps spinning, the leak is between the meter and the shutoff, in the yard line.

Where hidden leaks tend to hide

Different locations leave different clues. Matching the clue to the location narrows the search.

Inside walls and ceilings

Brown stains, bubbling paint, a musty smell or a warm patch on drywall. Bathroom supply lines and shower valves are frequent sources.

Under the slab

A warm spot on tile, the sound of water with everything off, or damp flooring along an outside wall. See slab leak repair.

Buried line from the meter

Wet ground between the meter and the house, a low-pressure complaint or a green strip of lawn. See water service line repair.

Irrigation lines

A zone that stays soggy after it shuts off, a broken head, or a valve box full of water. A leaking valve can quietly spin the meter.

Pool and spa lines

A pool that drops faster than evaporation explains, or constant auto-fill. Supply and return pipes underground can leak apart from the pool shell.

Toilet and fixture leaks

A toilet that runs on and off by itself, or a faucet that drips under the sink, can each add up to hundreds of gallons a month.

How we pinpoint a leak without tearing things open

We start with the plumbing's own information. A pressure test on the supply lines shows whether the pipe is losing pressure, and shutting zones off one at a time shows which section holds. Then we listen and scan.

Acoustic listening picks up the sound of water escaping a pressurized pipe. Thermal scanning picks up temperature differences from hot lines or wet material. Moisture meters check drywall and baseboards. No tool is perfect, and we may combine two or three to be sure. We do not name equipment brands because the method matters more than the label.

Irrigation, pool line and yard leaks

Sprinkler systems add many joints and valves, and pool lines run underground in plastic. We test each zone and the pool supply separately so the cost of the repair stays proportionate. If a leak is outside the building line, we explain what we can fix and what a pool or irrigation specialist would handle.

What insurance may and may not cover

Policies differ on hidden leaks. Some pay for a sudden break and the damage it causes, and many exclude long, slow seepage. Some cover the cost of locating the leak and some do not. Check your policy, call your insurer, and keep your meter readings, photos and any utility notices.

Finding a leak versus fixing it

Locating a leak is diagnostic work. Fixing it, once the pipe is exposed, is governed by the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which Florida builds on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), which is based on the IPC.

If the fix means opening a slab or replacing lines, ask the City of Greenacres building department about permits before the work starts.
