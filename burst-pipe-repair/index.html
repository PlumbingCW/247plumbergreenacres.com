Water pouring from a pipe: the first five minutes

Find the main shutoff

Most Greenacres homes have a valve on the front wall near the hose bib, or a box at the meter. Turn a ball valve a quarter turn, or a round gate valve clockwise until it stops.

Open the lowest faucet

Run a tub or outdoor spigot to drain the pipes. That cuts the flow at the break and drops the pressure.

Turn off power to wet areas

If water is falling near a panel, outlets or a ceiling fixture, switch off those breakers only if you can reach the panel while standing dry. Otherwise stay out and call.

Call our 24/7 line

Call us and say where the water is, whether it has stopped, and which walls are wet. That helps us send the right parts on the first trip.

A burst pipe in a Florida house

Our line is answered around the clock, and we book same-day appointments when available. A break in a wall, ceiling or slab can put hundreds of gallons into a house in a short time, so the shutoff matters more than anything you do after it.

Florida pipes rarely burst from ice. The usual causes here are age, corrosion on galvanized and old copper lines, pressure surges when a supply is turned on or off, tree roots pressing against buried pipe, and impact from yard work or a contractor's equipment. Plastic lines fail at fittings and clamps more than in the pipe body.

If the break is near electrical or a ceiling is sagging

Water and wiring do not mix. If a ceiling is bulging, do not poke it open to let the water out while standing under it. Keep away, shut off the main, and let us deal with the drain-down.

What to do before we arrive

A few quick steps limit the damage and make the repair faster.

Photograph the damage

Take clear photos of the wet area, the pipe and anything damaged. You may want them for an insurance claim later.

Move what you can

Lift rugs, electronics and cardboard boxes off wet floors. Wet drywall and insulation get heavy, so clear the room.

Keep the water off

Do not turn the main back on to test. Wait until we have capped or replaced the failed section.

Note what you saw

Tell us if you heard a bang, saw rust, or noticed the pressure change before the break. It points to the cause.

When it is not clear where the water is coming from

Some breaks are obvious, like a spray from a hose bib line. Others show up as a wet baseboard, a stain on the garage ceiling, or a meter that keeps turning with every fixture off. Water travels along framing and slab before it appears.

If you cannot see the break, we use leak detection to track it down before cutting into walls. That saves drywall and keeps the repair as small as the problem is.

What insurance may and may not cover

Policies often cover water damage from a sudden pipe failure but may exclude damage from slow leaks or a pipe that was clearly neglected. Terms differ, so check your policy and call your insurer. Keep photos, receipts and any damaged items until the claim is settled.

Code and permits after a burst

Replacing a failed section of supply line is plumbing work that falls under the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which Florida builds on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), built on the IPC. Emergency shutoff and drying come first, and the paperwork question comes right after.

Ask the City of Greenacres building department whether your repair needs a permit. It also helps to know that Greenacres requires a licensed contractor or an exempt owner-builder for permitted work.
