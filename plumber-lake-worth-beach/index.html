Old cottages, old pipes: what 1913 to 1949 construction means

Lake Worth Beach has six historic districts: College Park, Northeast Lucerne, Old Lucerne, Old Town, Southeast Lucerne and South Palm Park. About 1,000 of the cottages built between 1913 and 1949 still stand across them. A house that old has outlived its original plumbing at least once, so what sits under your sink is often a mix of three or four different eras.

Homes built before the 1950s were commonly plumbed with galvanized steel supply lines and cast iron drains. Galvanized pipe rusts from the inside until the opening narrows, which is why people describe weak pressure at one fixture or brown water after the house has sat quiet. Cast iron drains scale up and crack, and most of that pipe is hidden in walls and under floors where you cannot watch it fail.

We cannot tell you what is in your wall from the street. A camera down the drain and a pressure check on the supply side can. If the house has never been repiped, leak detection finds the active problem first, and repiping becomes a planned decision instead of a flood-day decision.

Four things older Lake Worth Beach plumbing tends to do

These are the patterns that show up again and again in houses from the bungalow era. None of them fixes itself, and each one gets more expensive the longer the water keeps moving.

Rusty water after a quiet day

Run a cold tap for 30 seconds in the morning. If it comes out brown and then clears, the inside of an old supply line is shedding rust. That is a sign the pipe wall is thinning, not just dirty.

One drain that keeps coming back

A snake opens a hole through the middle of a clog and leaves scale or roots on the pipe wall. When the same tub or sink slows again within weeks, a sewer inspection shows what the cable is missing.

Damp plaster or a soft floor edge

A pinhole leak at an old joint can weep for weeks inside a wall. Stained plaster, a musty closet or a warm patch on the floor all point to water you cannot see.

Low pressure at one tap only

If the rest of the house is fine, the problem is local: a narrowed pipe, a worn shutoff valve under the fixture, or a clogged aerator. A quick pressure check separates the three.

Before we arrive at your Lake Worth Beach cottage

Older houses have older shutoffs and older wiring. Take these four steps in order, and skip any that feel unsafe.

Close the main valve gently

Century-old gate valves can seize. Turn the handle clockwise slowly, and if it will not move, stop and tell us. Forcing it can snap the stem.

Keep power away from the water

Do not step into standing water to reach the breaker panel. If the floor is wet around the panel, leave it alone and mention it when you call.

Clear the floor and the ceiling below

Lift rugs and move furniture away from the wet area. If plaster ceiling sags below the leak, keep people out of that room.
