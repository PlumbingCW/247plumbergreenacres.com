Water backing up in the house right now

A drain that gurgles, gushes or pushes water back into a tub is an emergency, and our 24/7 line is answered around the clock. While you wait, stop running water in the house, keep the washer and dishwasher off, and keep everyone away from any wet outlets or cords on the floor.

If the washer drain is spilling or a toilet is rising, shut the toilet's supply valve behind the bowl and move rugs and electronics out of the way. Do not pour drain chemicals into a line that is already full. They sit in the standing water and can burn skin and eyes when we open the pipe.

Most calls we get fall into two groups. One fixture is slow or stopped, which usually means a clog in its own branch line. Several fixtures are slow at once, which points to the main drain, and that is a different job with a different machine.

How to tell a single clog from a main line problem

Where the water shows up tells you most of what you need to know before anyone arrives.

One sink or tub is slow

The blockage sits in that fixture's trap or its short branch line. Hair, soap scum and food scraps are the usual causes, and a hand snake clears most of them.

Toilet gurgles when the shower runs

Air is being pushed through a partly blocked line shared by both fixtures. Expect a clog further down the branch or in the main.

Tub fills when the washer drains

The lowest drain in the house takes the overflow first. That pattern points to the main line, not the tub.

Water or sewage in a floor drain

A floor drain in a garage or laundry room sits low on the system. Backup there means the main line is blocked or very slow.

Sewer smell near a drain

A dry trap, a cracked vent connection or a partly blocked line can all cause it. If the smell comes with slow drains, treat it as a blockage first.

Clog comes back within weeks

A repeat clog usually means grease build-up, roots or a bellied section of pipe. Cleaning alone only buys time until the cause is found.

Floor, tub, shower and kitchen drains all have different trap shapes and pipe sizes. A kitchen branch is typically a 2 inch line, while the main leaving the house is usually 4 inch. The tool and the approach change with each.

Kitchen sinks, toilets and main line backups

Callers describe the same handful of problems over and over. A kitchen sink drains slowly for a week and then stops. A toilet will not flush and gurgles. The washer backs up into the tub, and then the master bath starts to back up too.

Each of those has a different likely source, so the first questions we ask are where the water is going and which fixtures still work. Getting that picture over the phone helps us bring the right machine on the first trip.

Clogged kitchen sinks

Kitchen lines carry grease that cools and hardens on the pipe wall, so the opening shrinks a little more with every dishwashing. A snake that only pokes a hole through the middle gives you a few good weeks. We use a cutting head to scrape the wall, and we tell you if the disposal outlet or the dishwasher connection is part of the problem.

Toilets that will not clear

A toilet that rises and gurgles may have a clog in its own trap, or it may be reporting a blocked branch. If a plunger and an auger fail, we look for wipes, a toy or a problem at the closet bend. A toilet repair is sometimes the real fix when the bowl or flange is the cause.

Main line backups

When water comes up in the lowest tub or shower, the main is the suspect. We run a large cable from the cleanout, and if the cutter returns with roots or a wad of paper, we know what we are dealing with. If the cable stops short or keeps catching, that is the point to look with a camera.

Snaking, jetting or a camera: when to step up

A cable machine breaks through most clogs, but it leaves residue on the pipe wall. When that residue is the real problem, the next tool matters.

A snake or cable is enough when

  • One fixture is blocked and the others drain normally.
  • The clog is soft, like hair, paper or food.
  • This is the first time the line has stopped in months.
  • The cable pulls back clean after one or two passes.

Step up when

  • The same drain clogs again within weeks, so hydro jetting may be the better scouring tool.
  • The cable catches on something hard or stops at the same spot twice.
  • Several fixtures back up together, so a sewer inspection can show roots or a sag.
  • Sewer smell or damp ground in the yard suggests a break.

Jetting does not repair a cracked or collapsed pipe, and a cable can mask one. A camera pass is how we tell the difference, and it costs less than digging in the wrong place.

Why chemical cleaners cost more than they save

Liquid openers sit on top of a full blockage and do nothing until the water drops. They can soften old pipe joints and leave caustic water in the trap that splashes on whoever opens it. If you already poured some, tell us before we start so we can protect our hands and your fixtures.

What insurance may and may not cover

Homeowner policies generally treat a gradual blockage differently from a sudden backup, and many exclude sewer backup unless an endorsement was added. Policies differ, so check yours and call your insurer. Photograph any water damage before you clean up.

Keeping a Greenacres line clear after we leave

Greenacres homes tied to the county system send everything to a Palm Beach County Water Utilities main. Some older properties are still on septic, since the City's plan allows existing septic to remain until centralized service reaches them. Ask us which you have before any main line work, because a septic line has a tank and field that a sewer lateral does not.

A few habits matter more than any product. Wipe grease from pans into the trash instead of the sink, use a hair catcher in showers, and keep wipes labeled flushable out of the toilet. If your main keeps clogging, a scheduled sewer line cleaning is cheaper than a weekend emergency.

What the plumbing code says about drains

Drain, waste and vent piping falls under the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which Florida builds on the International Plumbing Code (IPC). The IPC covers how drains are sloped and vented, which is why a slow drain can trace back to venting and not just a clog.

Whether a job needs a permit depends on the work, so ask the City of Greenacres building department before you replace any section of drain pipe.
