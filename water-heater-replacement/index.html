Failed tank and a wet garage: what to do first

When a tank splits, it empties its whole volume onto the floor, and the damage grows with every minute of flow. Shut the cold water valve on the line above the heater first, then turn off the breaker or the gas control. Our line is answered around the clock, so call us once the water is stopped.

Stay out of standing water near any plugged-in equipment, and move boxes and stored items off the floor if it is safe. If there is a gas smell, leave the house and call the gas utility or 911 before anything else.

How a same-day tank swap runs

Stop the water and describe the unit

On the phone, tell us the fuel type, the tank size on the label if you can read it, and where the heater sits. That tells us what to bring.

Confirm the plan and the price

We look at the space, the venting, the shutoff valves and the drain pan before we quote. You hear the price before we touch anything.

Drain, disconnect and remove

The old tank is drained, the water, gas or electric connections are released and the unit is taken out of the space.

Set, connect, test and clean up

The new heater is set, connected, vented where needed and tested for leaks and temperature. Then we walk you through the shutoffs.

Permits, the Greenacres way

Replacing a water heater is a permitted job. For a like-for-like changeout, the City uses the Palm Beach County Universal Permit Application together with a Water Heater Changeout Form and the manufacturer specs, and applications are accepted electronically. When the work value reaches $5,000 or more, a Notice of Commencement is also needed.

Tankless and solar water heaters require plan check, which adds a step before installation. For specifics on your address, ask the City of Greenacres building department. Inspections are scheduled by 3:00 pm, so a late-day install may be inspected the next day.

Work must be done by a licensed contractor or an exempt owner-builder. You can confirm any contractor's license through the county search or the Florida DBPR Verify a Licensee page, and a Business Tax Receipt is not a license.

Signs the unit is past repairing

Sometimes the choice is made for you. These are the situations where a swap is the sensible move, and our repair service covers the ones that are not.

Water seeping from the tank body

A wet seam or a steady drip that does not come from a fitting means the shell has corroded through.

Rust-colored hot water

Brown water only from the hot taps points to the inside of the tank rotting out.

A rumbling tank with crusted sediment

Heavy scale on the floor of the tank wastes energy and often ends in a split seam.

Repeat repairs on an old unit

When parts keep failing on a tank that has already had a long service life, one more repair buys little.

Sizing the new heater

Tank size depends on how many people shower at once, how many bathrooms you have and whether a big soaking tub or a dishwasher shares the same morning. Replacing a 40 gallon tank with another 40 gallon tank is simple, but a household that has grown may be better served by a larger or a second option.

Fuel type is the other factor. Some callers ask about switching from gas to a heat pump unit, which changes the electrical load and the venting. We talk through those tradeoffs on the phone and again at the unit.

  • Number of people and bathrooms.
  • Peak morning demand.
  • Existing fuel and venting.
  • Space and clearance around the unit.
  • Electrical capacity for a switch to electric or hybrid.

What insurance may and may not cover

Policies treat a sudden tank rupture and slow seepage differently, so read your policy and call your insurer. Photograph the failed tank, the water damage and the data label before it leaves.

What happens to the old water heater

The failed tank leaves with us. It is drained, disconnected and carried out so you are not left with a rusted shell in the garage. Because it is bulky and heavy, we handle the removal instead of asking you to arrange it.

If you need more than a swap, such as a new location or added capacity, our installation service covers planned work. Homes with failing supply lines around the heater may also want a look at repiping.

Permit paperwork for a changeout

A replacement is governed by the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which Florida builds on the International Plumbing Code (IPC). In Greenacres, a like-for-like changeout uses the Palm Beach County Universal Permit Application and a Water Heater Changeout Form.

The forms call for manufacturer specifications, and work valued at $5,000 or more needs a Notice of Commencement. Tankless and solar units need a plan check first.
