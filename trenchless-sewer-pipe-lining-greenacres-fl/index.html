Trenchless Sewer Pipe Lining in Greenacres: How It Works and When It Fits

Lining can repair a cracked sewer pipe without trenching the yard. It is not right for every failure, and a camera tells you which kind you have.

By the Greenacres 24/7 Plumbing team.Published October 15, 2026.Updated October 15, 2026.

What lining does in one paragraph

Trenchless lining, often called cured-in-place pipe, repairs a damaged sewer line from the inside. A flexible resin-soaked liner is pulled or inverted into the old pipe, inflated against the walls and cured until it hardens into a new pipe within the old one. The yard stays mostly intact, with small access holes at the ends.

It works well on cracks, small gaps, root-damaged joints and worn pipe that still holds its shape. It does not work on a pipe that has collapsed, sagged badly or shifted apart at a joint. A camera pass decides which of those you have.

Start with a sewer inspection. The video tells you where the problem is, how long the damaged run is and whether lining is even on the table.

The lining process from first camera to final test

Camera inspection

A camera on a push cable travels the line from a cleanout. We record the run, mark depth and distance, and look for cracks, roots, offsets, bellies and breaks.

Clean the pipe

Roots, scale and grease must go. The surface needs to be clean and round for the resin to bond. Cutting and jetting are common here.

Install and cure the liner

The resin-soaked liner is placed in the pipe and expanded. Heat, steam or light cures it, and it hardens against the old wall.

Reopen and verify

Branch connections are reopened, and a second camera pass confirms the finished surface and flow. Ask for the footage.

The cure time depends on pipe length, diameter and method. Expect the sewer to be out of service while it cures, so plan around bathrooms and laundry.

Dig and replace vs trenchless lining

Neither is always better. The pipe's condition and the yard decide.

A belly, where the pipe sags and holds water, is the case that surprises people. Lining follows the shape of the pipe, so it will not correct a low spot. A camera shows whether the sag is a few inches or a long run.

When lining fits and when it does not

Lining is worth considering when

  • The pipe is cracked or leaking at joints but still round.
  • Roots keep returning at the same joints.
  • A trench would cross a driveway, patio or mature tree.
  • The camera shows clean access to the damaged run.

Plan on digging when

  • The pipe is collapsed, crushed or missing a section.
  • A sag holds water and the slope is wrong.
  • A joint has pulled apart and the pipes no longer line up.
  • The damaged run is short and easy to reach, so a sewer line repair by spot excavation costs less than lining.

Sewer, septic and drainage in Greenacres

The City of Greenacres long-range plan points to Palm Beach County Water Utilities and encourages converting septic tanks to centralized service. Existing septic may remain until centralized service is available. Before any sewer talk, find out whether your home is on a sewer lateral or a septic system, since lining applies to the pipe that runs to a sewer main, not to a drain field.

Digging in a yard brings drainage into the picture. The Lake Worth Drainage District and the South Florida Water Management District are the drainage authorities, and the City's plan requires single-family lots to retain the first inch of runoff. If a trench would cross a swale or change how water leaves your lot, think about that before you dig.

Permits are a separate question. Greenacres accepts permit applications electronically, and work must be done by a licensed contractor or an exempt owner-builder. For permit questions about sewer repair at your address, ask the City of Greenacres building department.

What shapes the cost of a sewer repair

Cost depends on the length of the damaged run, pipe diameter, depth, whether a cleanout exists, how much cleaning is needed first, what sits above the pipe and what restoration is required. Lining a long run under a driveway may still cost less than trenching, and a short spot dig in an open lawn may cost less than lining.

Ask what is included: the pre-camera, cleaning, lining, reinstating connections and the final camera pass. Ask how long the sewer will be out. We set a clear price before work starts, and the number comes from the video, not a guess. Call for your real number.

If a blockage is the only issue, sewer line cleaning may be all you need. Lining is for structural damage.

Questions to ask before you agree to lining

Ask to see the camera footage of your own line, not a stock video. Ask how the damage was measured and what the liner thickness will be. Ask how the finished pipe connects to the cleanout and the main, and what happens at the ends.

Verify the contractor too. Palm Beach County runs contractor certification, and you can check a Certificate of Competency through the county's licensed contractor search at pbc.gov/pzb. State licensing is through the Florida DBPR Verify a Licensee search at myfloridalicense.com. Warning signs include ads without a license number, large upfront payments and oral-only contracts.
